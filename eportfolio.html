Why Does Music Do Things to Us?

Music isn't just entertainment — it's one of the few things that touches almost every part of our brain at once. Have you ever gotten chills from a song, or cried while listening to a sad melody? That's not just emotional — it's neurological. Music can trigger memories, shift our mood, calm our stress, and even make us feel pain or pleasure more intensely.

💡 Example

That feeling when an old song comes on and suddenly you're right back in a specific moment from years ago — that's not a coincidence. It's your brain responding to a sound it stored alongside a memory.

❤️

🧠
Memory Centre

When your favourite song comes on, you might smile, remember something, and start moving — all at once!

Mood Manipulation — Happy vs. Sad Songs

Music has an incredible ability to change how we feel. This emotional manipulation is built right into the music through tempo, key, rhythm, and instrumentation. Major chords typically sound happy and uplifting, while minor chords can sound sad or melancholic.

😊
Major Chords
Happy, uplifting, energising
😢
Minor Chords
Sad, melancholic, reflective
💡 Example

Try watching a scary scene without music. It's way less scary!

06
Slide 6

Time Machine

"One note, one lyric — and you are back to a place, a person or a feeling. Songs act like memory keys to emotions we forgot."

Music is more than just sound — it's a kind of time machine. A few notes from a song can take you straight back to a specific moment in your past, whether it's a childhood lullaby, your school anthem, or a song that played during a big life event.

💡 Example

Imagine hearing a song from when you were 10 years old — maybe something that played during a family road trip. Suddenly, you remember the exact car ride, the way the sun felt on your face, and even the smell of the snacks. That's music working as a time machine in your brain.

07
Conclusion

Music Really Does Mess With Our Brain — But in a Good Way.

It makes us feel strong emotions, helps us remember the past, and even changes the way our brain works. It connects people, brings back memories, and makes life more enjoyable.

So next time you hear a song that gives you chills or takes you back in time, remember — your brain is doing something amazing.

Introduction

Mental health is a state of mental well-being that helps people to get through with the stresses of life, realise their worth and improve themselves. Unlike physical health, mental health has the tendency to be overlooked by people. Mental health is a factor that fluctuates depending on the current life circumstances. From childhood to adulthood, mental health has a big role to play in our lives. Everyone faces challenges and misadventures at some point in their lives that affect their mental state in one way or another where they may require attention and care.

Mental health is the cognitive core that rules how we assess reality, tackle stress and cruise through interpersonal relationships. Millions of people from all around the world are affected by mental health disorders which can manifest in multiple ways and are among the leading causes of disability. Essentially, people should be able to recognise how important mental health can be, not only at an individual level but as well as for the society and families.

Furthermore, mental health is closely linked with physical health. There is considerable evidence supporting the connection between mental state and diseases, such as cardiovascular diseases, pain and immune system disorders. For example, the effects of long-term stress on the body can lead to a number of diseases and exacerbate the symptoms of existing diseases.

The World Mental Health Today report in 2025 shows that over one billion people live with a mental health condition, yet most do not receive adequate care.

Mental Health Conditions

Mental disorders are conditions that affect your thoughts, behaviour, emotions and how you regulate them. They can be short term or long term. A person might experience more than a single mental health condition simultaneously. The degree that conditions affect a person differs from mild to severe.

😰 Anxiety Disorders Most common mental disorder

People with anxiety disorders may undergo excessive fear and stress about particular situations or a broad range of daily events, persisting for an extended period.

GAD — Constant fear, stress & feeling overwhelmed
Phobias — Anxiety triggered by specific situations
Panic Disorder — Recurring panic attacks or fear of them
Social Anxiety — Intense fear of being judged
Agoraphobia — Fear of being unable to escape
Selective Mutism — Inability to speak in certain environments
😔 Mood Disorders Severe mood imbalance

The primary symptom of mood disorders is a significant imbalance in mood. Depression affects people's lives in multiple ways — from long-term unhappiness and lack of hope, to losing interest in activities once appreciated.

🔮 Psychotic Disorders Dissociation from reality

Characterised by dissociation from reality with associated behavioural changes including delusions and hallucinations. Types include Schizophrenia, Schizoaffective disorder, Delusional Disorder, Brief Psychotic Disorder, and Shared Psychotic Disorder.

🍽️ Eating Disorders Abnormal eating behaviours

Characterised by abnormal eating behaviours and distorted body image. Includes Anorexia Nervosa (severe food restriction, fear of weight gain), Bulimia Nervosa (binge-purge cycles) and Binge Eating Disorder (uncontrolled excessive eating without purging).

💊 Substance Use Disorders Brain & behaviour impact

Refers to the abuse of substances leading to excessive use. Affects the brain and behaviour, making it difficult to control substance use. Includes alcohol use disorders and drug addiction. Individuals may develop tolerance and experience withdrawal symptoms.

🎭 Personality Disorders Three cluster types

Long-lasting, disruptive patterns of thinking, behaviour, mood and relating to others. Classified into three clusters:

CLUSTER A — Odd/Eccentric
Paranoid, Schizotypal & Schizoid Personality Disorders
CLUSTER B — Dramatic
Antisocial, Borderline, Histrionic & Narcissistic PDs
CLUSTER C — Anxious
Avoidant, Dependent & OC Personality Disorders

Mental Disorder Causes

The causes of mental disorders are not found in nature or nurture alone, but in the dynamic, lifelong dialogue between the two.

🧬

Genetics

A person can inherit genes that potentially increase the risk of certain mental health conditions — though having these genes does not guarantee development of a disorder.

⚗️

Brain Chemistry & Function

The brain uses chemicals called neurotransmitters to manage moods and behaviours. Any disturbance in how these chemicals function may result in mental disorders.

🌍

Life Experiences & Environment

Traumatic events, especially early in life, may increase the risk of a mental disorder. Factors like substance intake, unavailable social support and chronic medical conditions also play a role.

Risk Factors

A risk factor increases the chance that someone may develop a mental health problem. Having one or more risk factors does not guarantee a mental health issue — it increases the likelihood. These factors can be linked to personal, social, biological, or environmental influences.

🧬
Biological Risk Factors
Genetic predisposition and brain chemistry. A family history of Depression or Bipolar Disorder increases risk. Medical problems affecting the brain may also contribute.
🧒
Psychological Risk Factors
Childhood trauma, abuse, neglect or losing a parent. Negative self-image, distorted thought processes and emotional instability can all lead to disorders including Anxiety and personality issues.
🏙️
Social & Environmental Risk Factors
Stressful life events such as financial issues, academic pressure, unemployment or relationship troubles. Unsafe surroundings, discrimination, or lacking social support also affect mental well-being.
🛌
Lifestyle-Related Factors
Poor sleep, physical inactivity, improper diet or too much screen time may affect brain functions and emotions. Such lifestyles can result in stress and increase the risks of developing mental disorders.

Signs & Symptoms

Extreme mood swings — linked to difficulties regulating mood; associated with depression and anxiety.
Withdrawing from family/friends — a signal of anxiety issues or depression.
Feeling persistently down — key symptom of depressive disorders.
Low energy or sleep problems — caused by chemical imbalances in the brain.
Confused thinking or poor concentration — suggests impaired cognitive functioning.
Major changes in eating habits — could develop into an eating disorder if neglected.
Paranoia or hallucinations — may indicate serious conditions such as psychotic disorders.
⚠ Thoughts of self-harm or suicide — crucial warning signals requiring immediate attention.

Key indicator: When symptoms start disrupting daily activities, work, school life and relationships — or when emotions become too overwhelming and last for a long time — this is an indication of a mental health disorder. In some people, symptoms first appear as physical problems such as stomach aches, back pain or insomnia.

Impact of Poor Mental Health

Mental well-being is a critical factor that impacts not only individuals but also their family members and society at large. It affects people's thought processes, moods and behaviours, which may make it hard for them to perform day-to-day activities.

💔

Emotional & Psychological

Constant depression, irritation, anxiety and hopelessness. Negatively affects self-confidence and self-respect, creating a vicious cycle where poor mental state leads to an even worse perception of reality.

🏥

Physical Health

Insomnia, exhaustion, migraines and reduced immunity. Mental stress contributes to heart disease and hypertension. May lead to unhealthy coping behaviours such as substance abuse.

👥

Personal Relationships

Individuals may distance themselves from others, making social interaction difficult. Absence of social interaction further exacerbates the condition.

📚

Academic & Occupational

Difficulty keeping up with studies or job demands due to lack of concentration or motivation to perform.

Challenges in Mental Healthcare

1
Stigmatisation
Individuals do not seek assistance due to fear of judgement and labelling. People with Depression and Anxiety are often told to "just get over it" — avoiding necessary medical attention.
2
Limited Access & Cost
Fewer mental health practitioners, especially in rural areas. Services can be expensive and not covered by insurance, making them inaccessible to many.
3
Lack of Awareness & Education
Many are unfamiliar with psychological illnesses. Symptoms of disorders like Bipolar Disorder may go unnoticed due to their similarity to typical behavioural changes.
4
Social & Cultural Barriers
Cultural beliefs may lead people to prefer traditional remedies. Poverty and unemployment can worsen symptoms while limiting help-seeking behaviour.

Protective Factors

Mental health protective factors are characteristics or conditions that lessen the impact of stressors and reduce the likelihood of developing mental health concerns. They function as buffers across multiple levels — personal skills, social connections, and environmental stability.

🤝

Social Support

Supportive relationships with family & friends provide emotional support and reduce risk of depression.

💪

Emotional Resilience

Effective coping skills enable people to handle different problems and avoid developing anxiety disorders.

🏃

Physical Wellness

Exercise, balanced diet and adequate rest support brain function. Aerobic exercise boosts mood and energy.

🏠

Safe Environment

Living in a safe place, having education and belonging to a supportive community are important for mental well-being.

⭐

Self-Respect & Purpose

People with self-respect and purpose are far less prone to becoming demotivated. Hobbies, skills and goal-setting reinforce this.

Treatment & Care

Treatment aids the patient to cope with symptoms, better function in day-to-day activities and improve quality of life. This normally involves many factors, and the method used depends on the particular disorder or its severity.

🗣️
Psychotherapy (Talk Therapy)
Working with a trained professional to understand thoughts, emotions and behaviours. Includes Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) — which modifies negative thoughts — and Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT) for emotion regulation. Can be individual, group or family-based.
💊
Medication
Used to treat conditions such as depression and anxiety disorders by managing brain chemical levels. Most effective when integrated with psychotherapy techniques.
🏃
Lifestyle & Personal Care
Physical exercise, balanced nutrition and adequate rest considerably enhance mental health. Relaxation methods, maintaining a routine and avoiding negative stimuli such as substances facilitate recovery.
🤝
Social Support
Positive relationships with friends, family or social groups provide emotional support that prevents isolation. Good communication and a proper support network contribute to coping and adherence to treatment.

Self-Care

Self-care involves taking care of physical, mental, emotional and spiritual aspects of your life to stay healthy. Engaging in self-care activities helps stabilise emotions, boosts self-respect, and reinforces treatment.

💪
Physical
Plenty of rest, balanced diet, physical activity
💬
Emotional
Expressing emotions, talking to loved ones, journaling
📵
Mental
Breaks from stress, less screen time
🌐
Social
Healthy connections, clear boundaries with others

Conclusion

Mental health is an essential aspect of the general health of a human. Therefore, mental health should be treated with the same level of importance as physical health. This can happen by creating more awareness about mental health and eliminating the stigma associated with mental illness — helping individuals live healthier and more balanced lives.

Improving mental health involves individual actions as well as changes to systems and processes. Communities, schools, health services and governments all have a role to play in developing environments that are safe, connected and accessible.
